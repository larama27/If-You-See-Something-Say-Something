If You See Something, Say Something.

Something wrong. Something out of place. Someone getting hurt. This game trains your eyes to spot it — and your voice to speak up safely. You never have to fight, chase, or touch anyone. You just have to tell.

How you play

Watch each scene happen, spot what is wrong, and pick the safest thing to do. Every question starts with a 15-second timer at full points — answer before it runs out and you earn them all. After 15 seconds the points start dropping, so the slower you answer, the fewer points you get; if they drop all the way to zero, you still learn the safe move, but you score nothing. Three rounds — and every round gets harder than the one before. Two commercials between rounds give you a breather.

Step 1SEE it

Look close. What is happening? What does not belong? Trust your gut — if it feels wrong, it is worth noticing.

Step 2THINK — stay safe

Ask: “Is someone in danger? Can I help without getting close?” Never confront, chase, or grab. Your safety comes first, always.

Step 3SAY something

Tell a trusted adult, a teacher, a store worker — or call 911 if someone is in danger right now. Say what you saw, where, and when.

When you tell, tell these 4 things

Police and trusted adults can act fast when you give them this. You do not need to know a name. What you saw is enough.

The 4 W’s

  • What

    What happened?“A man grabbed a woman’s purse and ran.” Plain and simple.

  • Where

    Where did it happen?Street name, store, park, bus stop — the closest place you can name.

  • When

    When did it happen?“Just now.” “About 3 o’clock.” “Every day this week.”

  • Who

    What did the person look like?Tall or short, what they wore, hair, which way they went. Describe what you saw — never guess about a person’s race or where they are from.

Words you can use

To a trusted adult“I saw something that worries me. Can I tell you? I think someone needs help.”
Calling 911“My name is ___. I’m at ___. I saw ___. Someone needs help right now.” Then stay on the line and answer their questions.
If a friend tells you a bad secret“I believe you. It is not your fault. I’m going to tell a grown-up who can keep you safe — and I’ll keep telling until someone helps.”
If you are not sure“It might be nothing — but it didn’t look right, so I’m telling you.” Not sure is still worth saying.

Three things that are NEVER your job

  • Never confront or chase a person doing something wrong. Move away and tell.
  • Never keep a secret about someone being hurt or touched in a way that feels wrong — even if an adult told you to. Tell another trusted adult, and keep telling until you are believed.
  • Never blame the person being hurt. In domestic violence, stalking, and abuse, the fault belongs to the person doing the harm — nobody else.

If this is happening right now

This game is practice. Real life comes first. If someone is in danger right now, call 911. These free, private lines are real and open day and night in the United States:

911Someone is in danger right now, a crime is happening, or someone is badly hurt.
1-800-799-7233National Domestic Violence Hotline — for abuse at home or in a relationship. You can also text START to 88788.
1-800-422-4453Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline — if a child is being hurt, or you are a child who needs help.
988Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988 any time you or someone you know is in crisis.

If calling is not safe, tell a teacher, a nurse, a faith leader, a store manager, or another adult you trust. Keep telling until someone helps.
